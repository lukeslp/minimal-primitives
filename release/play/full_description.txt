Twenty-nine small puzzle games in one app that runs offline.

Interaction primitives are to doing what simple machines are to building: get, put, push, turn, join, split, bend, switch. Each game here is built on one or two of them.

* Hexsweeper: find the mines on hex tiles, on a sphere, a fixed board or an endless one (Sphere, Classic, Infinite)
* Notan: a black-and-white nonogram that reveals a small picture
* Akari: light every open square; bulbs must never shine on each other
* Spiral Galaxies: build shapes that match after a half-turn
* Bridges: join the islands and match each number
* Slant: a diagonal in every square, matching the numbers with no loops
* Fold: fold the paper until a dot shows through every hole
* Bending: move planets so their pull bends each beam into its light
* Tilt: tip stacked trays to drop the marble through the holes
* Horologist: place wheels so every clock runs forward
* Towers: raise towers so each row and column holds every height once and the edge numbers count the towers seen
* Tents: a tent for every tree, kept apart, matching the numbers on each row and column
* Constellations: join the stars of twelve real constellations until every star has its threads
* Shoo · Minimal: bees flee from wherever you tap; herd them home
* Balance: fill the grid with black and white, never three alike in a row
* Cast: stack cubes until three shadows match
* Tilt · 3D: tip a stack of glass trays so the marble drops through the right holes
* Cube Maze: tip a glass cube and turn its slices until the marble rolls out of the spout
* Tangles: turn tiles until every line closes into a loop, on hexagons and six other tilings
* Weaves: pass each thread over or under; none may float past two crossings
* Delve: descend a dungeon of squares, dodge attacks, fight and find the stairs

Motor-accessible puzzles, in their own grid:

* Glance: turn tiles over until every one is dark
* Glyph: trace a faint figure on a square of points
* Pursuit: follow a ring with a pointer until it comes to rest in the picture
* Shoo: herd the bees into the hive
* Nod: tip stacked trays with a drag, a swipe or the arrow keys
* Noon: a nonogram played with one switch, pressing as a cell's clock reaches noon
* Sweep: one switch and a highlight that sweeps the rows to turn tiles dark
* Ticker: one button, played by ear, pressing as each note of the tune comes around

Most games have an Access setting. One switch steps a highlight through the game's keys, in groups or one at a time; any key, tap or click is a press, and speed and timing can be changed. Look and hold puts the keys beside the board; rest a finger, mouse or head mouse on one until its ring fills. Glance, Glyph and Shoo also take a tap or a resting pointer on the board, Pursuit follows the pointer, Nod tips with a drag, a swipe or the arrow keys, and Noon, Sweep and Ticker take any key or a tap anywhere as their one switch.

Look and hold by head or eyes uses the front camera only if you turn it on in a game's Access settings; frames are processed on the phone and never saved or sent. No internet permission: nothing loads from the network, and links open in your browser. Saves and scores stay on the phone and out of cloud backup and device transfer. The other permission is vibration, for haptic feedback.
